8. In dino news, crocodile dinosaur turned into ostrich dino (by posture) [as it aged](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2518365-ancient-weirdo-reptile-graduated-from-4-legs-to-2-in-adolescence/)! Research done by Seattle's own Elliott Armour Smith, who has a name that rules.

9. Double dino day! We've got a new South Korean dino. [Meet Dooly](https://news.utexas.edu/2026/03/19/fossil-x-ray-reveals-new-species-of-baby-dino-named-for-iconic-korean-cartoon/)!

10. Children find mysterious Gaulish [sitting skeletons](https://people.com/children-find-skeleton-sitting-upright-near-playground-fifth-such-find-this-month-11930648).

11. For thousands of years, we didn't know how eels reproduce. Spontaneous spawning was a popular theory. Turns out (as we learned in 2022), they [grow genitals](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/fish/eel-reproduction-mystery) when near their secret spawning grounds. There's also some pokemon-style evolution along the way.

12. Related, locusts are grasshoppers that have a [swarming phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust) when their populations get too high. 

13. Sam Dunnewold of Dice Exploder: [12 questions](https://diceexploder.com/posts/a-dozen-ish-questions-for-designing-your-game) to ask when making your game.
