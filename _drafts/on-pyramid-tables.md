Blog on pyramid tables
      Growing up, my father had a lamp in his living room. To prevent glare, he installed a light shield almost entirely around the bulb. (drawing). This makes it impractical to read under, unless you were constantly shifting your book to align with the thin halo of light. 

      You can design a table that doesn't gather water marks (crumbs*). Just make it into a sharp pyramid. (drawing)
      Oh, what's that? It's no longer useful as a table?

      Sometimes we forget that design is a tradeoff. 

      [computer] Security is a balance between usability and [thesaurus security]. You can prevent users from copy pasting, but they can always pull out another device and take a picture. You're just blocking honest people from doing their jobs.

        Pyrmaid tables add that there's a tradeoff between security and usability. The most secure system is one no one can access. But even if you disable copy/paste, wall off USB ports, and have three-factor biometics, someone can still take a picture of the screen. You lock phones at the door, and you're fighting memory. All you've done is make the user experience terrible for the people you like, and if you don't trust them, what are you doing?
